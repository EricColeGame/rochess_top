## Picking a Time Control That Fits How You Think

Once you understand the basics of RoChess how to play, the clock becomes the next decision. The game groups its nine time controls into the familiar bullet, blitz, and rapid families, with separate leaderboards for each plus a puzzle ladder.

| Time control family | Pace | Best for | Leaderboard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bullet | Very fast, seconds per side | Reflex training and quick breaks | Yes |
| Blitz | Fast, a few minutes per side | The most popular rated format | Yes |
| Rapid | Slower, more thinking time per move | Learning openings and calculation properly | Yes |
| Puzzles | Untimed per position | Daily tactics training | Puzzle leaderboard |

A practical rule: play rapid while you are still learning, blitz once your openings feel automatic, and bullet only for fun. Bullet is the fastest way to build bad habits because you physically cannot calculate — you pattern-match and hope. If you want your rating to rise, spend at least half your games at the slower controls where thinking is still possible.

Also resist playing all nine controls at once. Splitting your attention across every time control spreads your rating thin and makes it hard to tell whether you are actually improving.

## Bots, Puzzles, and Openings: Building a Training Routine

Most RoChess how to play guides stop at "play more games." That advice is incomplete. Games test you; training changes you. The game gives you three training tools — bots, puzzles, and opening recognition — and they serve different purposes.

| Training stage | Which bots to face | What you are practicing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Absolute beginner | The lowest-rated bots on the list | Piece movement, not leaving pieces hanging |
| Novice | The next tier up | Basic tactics, king safety, castling early |
| Intermediate | The middle of the 19-bot ladder | Opening plans and simple endgames |
| Advanced | The strongest bots | Calculation depth and time management |
| Master | The hardest bots available | Full-game pressure with no mistakes allowed |

Treat the bot ladder as a staircase, not a buffet. Beat a tier consistently before moving up, and if you lose three in a row, drop back down. Community reports suggest that players who grind the bot ladder methodically reach competitive online play faster than those who jump straight to rated games.

Puzzles deserve their own slot in your routine. With 1,300+ rated puzzles, you can build a genuine daily habit: ten puzzles before your first game, every session. Puzzle rating is also its own leaderboard, so improvement there is measurable and separate from your playing rating.

Finally, let the opening recognition do some work for you. With 3,800+ openings tracked as you play, you will start seeing names attached to the lines you already play — which makes it much easier to look up theory on the ones you keep losing.

## Game Review and the Analysis Board: Where Ratings Actually Come From

The part of RoChess how to play that players skip most often is the review. After a game ends, the Game Review tool produces an accuracy score and highlights your brilliant moves alongside your blunders. That is a diagnostic report on your own thinking, and it is free to read.

| Feature | What it does | Best time to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Game Review | Accuracy score, brilliant moves, blunders | Immediately after every rated game |
| Analysis Board | Live engine on any position | When you cannot see why a move was bad |
| Opening recognition | Names the openings you played | Post-game, to look up the line |
| Puzzle rating | Tracks tactics progress | Daily, before playing |

Use them in that order. Review first, then open the Analysis Board only for the two or three positions you genuinely did not understand. If you run the engine on every move, you learn nothing — you just watch a machine agree with itself.

One more habit worth building: write down (or mentally note) the single move in each game that lost you the most accuracy. Over twenty games, patterns appear — a tendency to trade into worse endgames, or to push pawns in front of your king. Fixing one repeated mistake is worth more than memorizing ten opening lines.

## RoChess How to Play Tips That Speed Up Your Progress

These are the habits that separate players who climb the leaderboards from players who plateau at the same rating for months.

- **Play a fixed warm-up.** Ten puzzles, then one bot game, then rated games.
- **Cap your session.** Three to five rated games is plenty; fatigue causes blunders.
- **Never queue angry.** After two losses in a row, stop and review instead.
- **Pick two openings and stick to them.** One as white, one as black.
- **Use the slower controls to learn, the faster ones to test.**
- **Check the leaderboards weekly, not hourly.** Rating moves in trends, not single games.

| Week | Focus | Daily routine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Stop hanging pieces | 10 puzzles, 2 bot games, review both |
| 2 | Clock discipline | 10 puzzles, 2 rapid games, review both |
| 3 | Opening consistency | 10 puzzles, 1 bot game, 2 rapid games |
| 4 | Convert advantages | 15 puzzles, 3 games, full review of losses |

That four-week structure is deliberately boring, and that is the point. Improvement in chess comes from repetition with feedback, not from volume.

## Frequently Asked Questions About RoChess

**Do I need to know chess already to start playing?**
No. The game explicitly points new players toward the easy bots first, and you can learn piece movement, castling, and basic checkmates without ever risking a rating. Once you can finish a bot game without blundering a piece, move to rated play.

**Is the core of RoChess how to play different on mobile?**
The core is the same across devices: pick a time control, play, review. What changes is the interface — touch controls on a phone make fast bullet games harder, so most mobile players stick to rapid and puzzles.

**How long does a typical game take?**
It depends entirely on the time control family. Bullet games finish in well under a minute, blitz in a few minutes, and rapid games can run considerably longer. If you only have five minutes, choose a fast control rather than abandoning a slow game.

**Does playing RoChess actually improve my real chess?**
Yes, if you use the tools. Rated games build experience, puzzles build pattern recognition, and Game Review plus the Analysis Board build understanding. Players who only play games and never review tend to improve far more slowly — the review is where the learning happens.

RoChess how to play comes down to a short list: learn the board against a bot, choose a time control you can actually think in, train with puzzles daily, and review every rated game before you queue the next one. The tools are already built into the experience — the only variable is whether you use them.
