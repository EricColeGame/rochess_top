## Time Controls: Choosing the Right Clock for Bot Games

RoChess supports the standard chess spread of Bullet, Blitz, and Rapid, and each one teaches something different when you are facing a RoChess chess bot rather than a person. Bullet sharpens pattern recognition and premove instincts. Blitz is the sweet spot for most improvers. Rapid is where you can actually think about the position instead of reacting to it.

| Format | Typical Clock | What It Trains | Bot Match Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bullet | Under 3 minutes | Instinct, flagging awareness | Blunder spirals, no learning |
| Blitz | 3–10 minutes | Calculation under mild pressure | Rushed endgames |
| Rapid | 10–60 minutes | Full thought process, plans | Overthinking simple wins |
| Puzzles | Untimed | Tactical pattern bank | None — pure repetition |

A simple rule from player experience: if you cannot explain why you made your last three moves, the clock was too fast for that session. Drop down a format instead of pushing through.

### A Bot Session That Actually Teaches You Something

1. Open RoChess and head to the play menu.
2. Choose a bot game in a format one step slower than your comfort zone.
3. Play the same opening in three straight games.
4. Review accuracy immediately after each game.
5. Note one recurring mistake and repeat the session the next day.

That structure turns scattered bot games into a drill. Three games on the same line beats ten random ones.

## Five Habits That Beat the RoChess Chess Bot

Bots do not tilt, they do not get bored, and they do not hand you free pieces out of frustration. That means the habits that beat human opponents — baiting, time pressure, psychological pressure — do not transfer. What transfers is discipline.

- **Trade into simpler positions when you are up material.** Fewer pieces means fewer chances to misjudge a tactic.
- **Check every capture before you play it.** Most bot losses in community reports come from a single uncalculated exchange.
- **Play the position, not the highlight reel.** Bots punish speculative sacrifices far more reliably than casual human opponents.
- **Slow down in the endgame.** King and pawn technique is where improvers bleed the most half-points.
- **Stop after two losses in a row.** Fatigue is the real opponent by game three.

| Common Mistake | What It Costs | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Autopiloting the opening | Repeats the same bad structure | Pick one line and drill it |
| Ignoring king safety | Quick tactical losses | Castle before move 12 in most games |
| Playing on tilt | Rating slide | Hard stop after two losses |
| Skipping the review | Same error next session | Read the accuracy report every game |
| Only playing fast formats | Shallow calculation | One Rapid session per week |

None of these are exotic. They are the boring habits that separate a 900 from a 1400.

## Reading Your Accuracy Report After a Bot Match

The post-game review is the most underused mechanic in the game. RoChess shows your accuracy after a match, and that single number is more useful than the result itself — a win at 55% accuracy teaches you less than a loss at 85%.

| Metric | What It Tells You | What To Do About It |
|---|---|---|
| Overall accuracy | How often you matched strong moves | Track it across ten games, not one |
| Opening phase accuracy | Whether your repertoire is sound | Drill the line or replace it |
| Middlegame accuracy | Calculation quality | Slow down; check forcing moves |
| Endgame accuracy | Technique and conversion | Study basic king and pawn endings |
| Blunder count | Where games are lost | Review those exact positions first |

Community reports suggest accuracy varies noticeably between formats, so compare like with like — do not measure a Bullet number against a Rapid number and conclude you got worse.

One more mechanic worth using: puzzles. Solving them between bot games builds the tactical pattern bank that shows up in your blunder count within a couple of weeks.

## FAQ

**Does playing the RoChess chess bot affect my rating?**
It depends on the mode you select. Check the label on the match before you start — if you want a risk-free experiment, keep your untested openings for practice sessions and save your main repertoire for rated games.

**Is beating the RoChess chess bot easier than beating a human?**
Not necessarily. A bot does not blunder from frustration or run out of ideas, so it punishes loose play more consistently. The trade-off is that it also never surprises you with a wild, unbooked sacrifice.

**How many games should I play against the RoChess chess bot per day?**
Three to five focused games with a review after each beats twenty autopiloted ones. Quality of attention is the limiting factor, not volume.

**Why do I keep losing to the RoChess chess bot in Bullet?**
Bullet rewards pattern recognition you have not built yet. Move up one format, fix the recurring blunder the accuracy report keeps flagging, then come back to Bullet once the patterns are automatic.
